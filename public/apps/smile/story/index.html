01

Brief

A dental clinic doesn't sell visits, it sells treatments that take several sittings. A root canal and a crown are three appointments over three weeks; an implant is three over three months; aligners run for a year. Most clinics in Bengaluru keep this in an appointment diary, a paper case sheet with a hand-drawn tooth diagram, a receipt book and the dentist's memory. So the patient who skipped sitting 2 is noticed only when the crown comes back from the lab, the “₹15,000 in three parts” is a guess at the desk, consent is a signature on a form nobody can find, the X-ray lives on the sensor's laptop, and the six-month check-ups that keep a practice full are forgotten.

SmileOS runs the clinic around the treatment plan. The patient books a slot by work, dentist and chair, with booked times closed; reception confirms it and reminders go out. The dentist examines on a real 32-tooth chart of both jaws, where each tooth shows its colour per work (done, planned, in progress) and its whole history in a tap, records findings and adds work per tooth. The plan with cost per tooth is split into sittings automatically and sent to the patient's phone, who approves it and signs the consent on a canvas.

Every sitting moves the progress (“sitting 1 of 3”), books the next one, and sends the crown or aligners to the dental lab, tracked until they are fitted. Instalments are scheduled with the plan and paid by UPI with a receipt; the X-ray and photo gallery, prescriptions from templates and the six-month recall list keep the patient coming back. The owner sees chairs, collections, plans and the balance still to come live, with each dentist's commission and both branches side by side.

Names considered

FrameMind SmileOS says what a patient comes for and what a dental clinic sells, not the drill. It reads well on a waiting-room poster and a reminder SMS, fits a one-chair practice as well as a chain, and follows the family rule (FrameMind <Word>OS, “OS” in the accent).

  • SmileOS — chosen — the outcome patients want, in one friendly word
  • DentOS — considered — clear to dentists, but cold for patients and close to “dent” (damage)
  • ChairOS — considered — names the furniture, not the care; confusing outside the clinic

Architecture

  • Dentist → chair → slot. Each dentist works on a chair in a branch. A slot is 30 minutes; a booking takes as many slots as the work needs and is closed if either the dentist or the chair is busy, so two receptionists can't double-book a chair. App bookings wait as requests until the desk confirms them; walk-ins go straight onto the chair.
  • The chart is worked out, not drawn. Teeth use FDI numbers (11–48). What a tooth shows comes from its findings and every plan item on it: filled with the colour of the last work done, outlined in the colour of the work still to do (dashed when planned, thick when in progress), a red dot for an open finding, dashed when missing. The history is the same records, newest first, with that tooth's X-rays.
  • Plan → sittings → instalments. Each kind of work has its steps (a root canal two, a crown two, an implant three). Sittings are built from the steps: single-step work goes in sitting 1, multi-step work runs side by side, and a crown on a root-canal tooth starts in the canal's last sitting. Plans over ₹10,000 are paid 40 / 30 / 30; what is paid is the sum of receipts, never a stored balance, so the patient, the desk and the owner always agree.
  • Lab and consent. A sitting with an impression or scan step offers to send that item to the dental lab (shade included); the order moves sent → at the lab → back at clinic, and is closed when the fitting sitting is finished. Works that need consent (root canal, extraction, surgery, implant, aligners) are signed on a canvas, on the phone or at the desk, and the signature prints on the consent form.
  • Static pages only. Built with Next.js on FrameMind's shared core as a static export, served from framemindaistudio.com/apps/smile. No server and no sign-up: the demo keeps sample data in the visitor's browser, updates other tabs live and starts a fresh day each morning. A paying clinic gets a real backend behind the same data interface, its X-ray sensor's images, a UPI gateway and WhatsApp.
  • Shared core. Dummy logins and route guards for four roles, feature switches with tiers, demo UPI payments, printable receipts, plans, prescriptions, consent forms and day reports, simulated SMS / WhatsApp, and English / हिन्दी / ಕನ್ನಡ.
