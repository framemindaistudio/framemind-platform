Brief

A coaching centre in Bengaluru runs on a register, a fee book and a dozen WhatsApp groups. Enquiries come by walk-in, phone and Instagram and live in the counsellor's diary; the free demo class is promised and forgotten. Admission forms are photocopies, batches overflow because nobody counted the seats, and the fee plan with its instalments and the “sibling discount” is worked out on paper each time. Teachers call the roll aloud, and the parent hears their child bunked a class a month later. Notes go out as phone photos, doubts are asked in the corridor, weekly tests are printed, marked by hand and ranked in a spreadsheet nobody sends home, and the desk spends the last week of every month phoning parents about fees.

ClassOS runs the whole year in one app. The enquiry is logged with counselling notes and a follow-up date; the student books a free demo class from the centre's website, or the desk books one, and the teacher sees the demo student in that day's class. The online admission form brings the student's details and documents; the desk verifies them and allots a batch — a full batch can't take one more. The fee plan splits the course fee into instalments with a capped concession, and the parent pays each one by UPI with a GST receipt. Teachers take attendance by roll call or a class QR the students scan; an absent student's parent is alerted at once.

Teachers upload notes and video lectures (watermarked, with DRM simulated in this demo), schedule live classes, set assignments that students submit and teachers grade, and answer the batch's doubt box. Online MCQ tests are built from the question bank and really run: a timer, answers saved as you tap, automatic submission at zero, +4/−1 marking, then the rank, percentile and topic-wise analysis for the student, the parent and the teacher. Written tests get their marks uploaded and ranked the same way. Parents see attendance, marks, assignments, the report card and certificates, get fee reminders, and accept the next course with an early-bird concession. The owner watches both branches live: admissions, collection, dues ageing, attendance, results by batch and teacher pay by hours or batches.

Architecture

  • Course → batch → timetable. A course carries its fee and its concession limit; a batch belongs to a course and a branch and has a seat limit; weekly timetable slots give each class a day, a time, a subject and a teacher. Seats taken are counted from the students in the batch, never stored, so admissions, the desk and the owner can't disagree.
  • One record from enquiry to renewal. An enquiry carries its counselling notes, follow-ups and demo class; the admission form links to it; admitting creates the student in the allotted batch; the fee plan, receipts, attendance, submissions, test attempts, certificates and the next-course offer all point at that student.
  • Marks are worked out, not typed. An online attempt stores only the option picked for each question. Marks (+4 / −1), rank (one more than the number who scored higher), percentile (share of the others who scored lower) and topic-wise accuracy are computed from the answers and the question bank every time, so a late submission re-ranks everyone at once. Written tests store marks and rank the same way.
  • Money rules in one place. Coaching fees include 18% GST; the receipt shows the taxable value and CGST / SGST. A concession is a fixed rule (sibling 5%, merit 10%, staff ward 15%, full payment 5%) or a special amount, always capped at the course's limit. The net fee is split into equal instalments (the last takes the rounding); an instalment is due within 7 days, overdue after its date, and reminders go to the parent. Teacher pay is hours taught × rate, or batches taught this month × the batch rate, straight from attendance.
  • Static pages only. Built with Next.js on FrameMind's shared core as a static export, served from framemindaistudio.com/apps/class. No server, no sign-up: the demo keeps sample data in the visitor's browser and updates other tabs live; a paying centre gets a real backend behind the same data interface, a UPI gateway, a DRM video host and Zoom / Meet accounts.
  • Shared core. Dummy logins and route guards for five roles, feature switches with tiers, demo UPI payments, printable receipts, fee statements, report cards, rank lists and certificates, simulated WhatsApp to parents, and English / हिन्दी / ಕನ್ನಡ.
