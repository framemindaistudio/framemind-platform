01

Brief

A diagnostic lab sells a promise: the right tube from the right person reaches the right machine, and a signed report reaches the patient and the doctor on time. Most labs in Bengaluru run that on a booking phone, a notebook of home visits, hand-written tube labels, a technician typing values off the analyser screen and a pathologist signing printouts. So the patient doesn't know when the collection partner is coming, tubes get mixed up, a high sugar goes out without the last three reports beside it, the doctor who referred the patient hears nothing, reports breach their promised time unnoticed, and the nursing home on credit hasn't paid since August.

SampleOS tracks every sample from booking to report. The patient books a test or package with the price upfront, at home in a collection slot or at a centre, and pays by UPI. A collection partner is assigned automatically; the patient watches the ETA, and the partner can only draw blood after entering the sample code from the patient's phone. Each tube gets a real Code 128 barcode made from the sample id, and the lab receives it with one scan, which starts its turnaround clock.

The technician enters results with normal ranges (or pulls them from the analyser, simulated here), and high, low and critical values are flagged as they're typed. The pathologist sees every value against the patient's past results, sends a test back for a rerun or signs; the report goes to the phone as a PDF on the lab's letterhead with the signature, with trends over past reports, and to the referring doctor's portal. The owner sees the lab live, dues, daily and monthly reports, B2B clients on credit, doctors' commissions and the collection centres side by side.

Architecture

  • Booking → tubes → samples. A booking holds packages and single tests; tests expand without duplicates, and each test names its tube (EDTA, serum, fluoride, urine). Collection makes one sample per tube type with a barcode built from the booking number and the tube letter, drawn as real Code 128 (start code, data, modulo-103 check, stop) so a lab scanner reads the printed label.
  • Slots and partners. Home collection runs in half-hour slots per centre, two bookings a slot; past and full slots are closed. A new home booking goes to a partner of that centre who covers the patient's area and has the fewest jobs that day. The 4-digit sample code is made at booking and checked at the door, so the right person gives the sample.
  • Results are checked, not trusted. Every parameter carries its reference interval (with women's ranges where they differ) and critical limits. Flags (H, L, critical, abnormal) are worked out from the value every time, never stored, so the technician, pathologist, patient, doctor and PDF always agree. Trends are the same parameter across the patient's signed reports.
  • Turnaround and sign-off. A sample's clock starts when it is scanned at the lab: due = received + the slowest test's promised hours. Late and due-within-the-hour samples raise alerts. A report is released only when every test of the booking is signed; a critical value also alerts the referring doctor.
  • Money. What a patient owes is the bill minus receipts, never a stored balance. B2B clients get their own discount and are billed on credit: balance = unpaid before this month + this month's bookings − payments, against a credit limit. Referral commission is a percentage of the bill without the home-collection charge.
  • Static pages only. Built with Next.js on FrameMind's shared core as a static export, served from framemindaistudio.com/apps/sample. No server and no sign-up: sample data lives in the visitor's browser, other tabs update live, and a fresh day starts each morning. A paying lab gets a real backend behind the same data interface, its analysers connected (ASTM / HL7), a UPI gateway and WhatsApp.
06

Workflow

  1. 1. Test or package booked, home or lab visit
  2. 2. Collection partner assigned; patient tracks arrival
  3. 3. Sample barcoded and received at the lab
  4. 4. Results entered with normal ranges; abnormal values flagged
  5. 5. Pathologist checks and signs
  6. 6. Report PDF on the phone and WhatsApp; trends over time
