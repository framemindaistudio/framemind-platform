Brief

A busy family clinic in Bengaluru runs its day on a register at the desk and a stack of paper slips. Patients phone to “book”, then come at nine anyway, because nobody can tell them when their turn will be; by ten the waiting room is full of people who could have stayed home for another hour. The desk shouts numbers, the doctor asks “who's next?”, a patient who stepped out to the pharmacy loses their place, and the evening session overbooks because nobody counted the morning's phone calls. The prescription is handwritten, the pharmacy guesses at it, the lab report comes back on WhatsApp to the doctor's personal phone, the follow-up date is on a slip the patient loses, and at night the owner adds up the cash book to know what the day made.

TokenOS runs the day as one live queue. The patient books a token from home for a doctor's morning or evening session; each session has a daily token limit and booking closes by itself when it is reached. The phone then shows the live queue: the number now with the doctor, people ahead and an estimated wait worked out from that doctor's minutes per patient. At the clinic, reception marks the patient arrived, records vitals, adds walk-ins and phone bookings to the same queue, and marks no-shows. The waiting-room TV shows every doctor's running token and the next numbers, and flashes the token and room number when the doctor calls.

The doctor calls the next patient who has actually arrived in one tap, can skip, hold and recall, and sees the history, vitals and reports. Visit notes, a diagnosis and an e-prescription from templates, lab tests for the partner lab and the follow-up date go in on one screen; finishing the visit raises the bill, sends the prescription on WhatsApp and to the clinic pharmacy, which dispenses from stock at MRP. The patient pays by UPI in the app, the desk reminds follow-ups, and the owner watches the day report (patients, no-shows, waits and collections by doctor and branch) update as it happens. Video consults and ABHA linking are in the demo as simulations.

Names considered

FrameMind TokenOS names the one thing every Indian clinic patient already understands: the token. It promises the end of the guesswork about when your number comes, fits a single-doctor OPD as well as a multi-doctor clinic, and follows the family rule (FrameMind <Word>OS, “OS” in the accent).

  • TokenOS — chosen — the word patients and receptionists already use
  • CareOS — taken — and too broad: reads as home care or a hospital suite
  • ClinicOS — taken — and says where it runs, not what it fixes

Architecture

  • Doctor → session → token. A doctor belongs to a branch and has sessions (morning, evening) with a start, an end and a daily token limit. A token is a number in one doctor's session on one day, from the app, a walk-in or a phone booking. Tokens used are counted from the queue, never stored, so the booking screen, reception and the owner can't disagree, and booking closes the moment the count reaches the limit.
  • The queue is worked out, not kept. “Now running” is the token with the doctor (or the last one seen); the next patient is the lowest number that has arrived, so people who booked but aren't here are passed over without being lost; “people ahead” is everyone still waiting with a lower number plus the one inside, and the wait is that times the doctor's minutes per patient. The patient's phone, the desk, the doctor and the TV all compute it from the same tokens, so a tap anywhere moves all of them at once.
  • One visit record. Calling a token opens a visit; vitals and reports recorded at the desk carry into it. Finishing it stores the notes, diagnosis and prescription lines, raises one bill (consultation fee plus any lab tests), sends a prescription order to the pharmacy, places the lab order with the partner lab, schedules the follow-up and sends the prescription on WhatsApp. The lab's report comes back as an attachment on the same visit.
  • Money rules. Consultation and diagnostic services are GST-exempt, so their bills carry no tax. Medicines are billed at MRP with GST included; dispensing takes stock down by tablets per day × days (packs for syrups and creams), never below zero, and flags anything at or under its reorder level. Every payment records its method, so the day report splits collections by consultation, lab, pharmacy and UPI / card / cash.
  • Static pages only. Built with Next.js on FrameMind's shared core as a static export, served from framemindaistudio.com/apps/token. No server, no sign-up: the demo keeps sample data in the visitor's browser, updates other tabs live, and starts a fresh day each morning. A paying clinic gets a real backend behind the same data interface, a UPI gateway, a video service, a lab integration and ABDM registration for ABHA.
  • Shared core. Dummy logins and route guards for six roles, feature switches with tiers, demo UPI payments, printable bills, prescriptions and day reports, simulated WhatsApp, and English / हिन्दी / ಕನ್ನಡ.

Workflow

  1. 1. Patient books a token from home, or reception adds a walk-in
  2. 2. Live queue on the phone: people ahead and wait time
  3. 3. Doctor calls the next patient; notes, vitals and prescription
  4. 4. Prescription on WhatsApp; follow-up date set
  5. 5. Bill and payment; pharmacy dispenses
  6. 6. Follow-up reminder; owner's day report
